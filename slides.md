# Principles of Antimicrobial Naming: USAN and WHO INN
<div class="text-slate-500 dark:text-slate-400 text-base -mt-1 mb-3">Balancing structural heritage, global consistency, and medication safety</div>

<!-- Visual Nomenclature Equation Box -->

  <div class="px-3 py-1 rounded-lg bg-amber-100 dark:bg-amber-900/50 text-amber-900 dark:text-amber-200 border border-amber-200 dark:border-amber-700 font-bold flex items-center gap-1.5">

    <span>💊</span> <span>Approved Generic Name</span>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="grid grid-cols-2 gap-4 text-base">
  <div class="space-y-3.5">
    <div class="p-3.5 rounded-xl bg-blue-50 dark:bg-blue-950/40 border-l-4 border-l-blue-500 border-blue-200 dark:border-blue-800">
      <div class="flex items-center gap-2.5 mb-1.5">
        <div class="w-8 h-8 rounded-lg bg-blue-100 dark:bg-blue-900/60 text-blue-800 dark:text-blue-200 flex items-center justify-center text-base shrink-0">🌐</div>
        <div class="font-bold text-blue-900 dark:text-blue-200 text-lg">Traditional vs. Modern Governance</div>
      </div>
      <div class="leading-relaxed text-gray-700 dark:text-gray-200">
        <ul class="list-disc pl-5 space-y-1.5">
          <li><b>Traditional Naming:</b> Early agents were named organically from discovery sources (producing organisms) or chemical structures.</li>
          <li><b>Modern Standardization:</b> Overseen by the <b>USAN Council</b> in coordination with WHO <b>INN Programme</b> to ensure global consistency.</li>
        </ul>
      </div>
    </div>

    <div class="p-3.5 rounded-xl bg-amber-50 dark:bg-amber-950/40 border-l-4 border-l-amber-500 border-amber-200 dark:border-amber-800">
      <div class="flex items-center gap-2.5 mb-1.5">
        <div class="w-8 h-8 rounded-lg bg-amber-100 dark:bg-amber-900/60 text-amber-800 dark:text-amber-200 flex items-center justify-center text-base shrink-0">🧩</div>
        <div class="font-bold text-amber-900 dark:text-amber-200 text-lg">Suffix and Prefix Dynamics</div>
      </div>
      <div class="leading-relaxed text-gray-700 dark:text-gray-200">
        <ul class="list-disc pl-5 space-y-1.5">
          <li><b>Class Suffix (Stem):</b> Preserved across new drugs in the same class to denote shared mechanism and molecular core.</li>
          <li><b>Distinct Prefix:</b> Proposed by developers, typically <b>2 syllables</b> to ensure distinct phonetic separation and avoid medication confusion.</li>
        </ul>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="space-y-3.5">
    <div class="p-3.5 rounded-xl bg-rose-50 dark:bg-rose-950/40 border-l-4 border-l-rose-500 border-rose-200 dark:border-rose-800">
      <div class="flex items-center gap-2.5 mb-1.5">
        <div class="w-8 h-8 rounded-lg bg-rose-100 dark:bg-rose-900/60 text-rose-800 dark:text-rose-200 flex items-center justify-center text-base shrink-0">🛡️</div>
        <div class="font-bold text-rose-900 dark:text-rose-200 text-lg">Regulatory Rules & Excluded Letters</div>
      </div>
      <div class="leading-relaxed text-gray-700 dark:text-gray-200">
        Strict criteria enforced by USAN and WHO INN:
        <ul class="list-disc pl-5 mt-1 space-y-1">
          <li><b>No outcome promises:</b> Cannot promise efficacy (e.g., no <i>"Cureall"</i>).</li>
          <li><b>No medical terminology:</b> Avoid anatomical or disease references.</li>
          <li><b>Excluded Letters (H, K, J, W, Y):</b> Avoided because they are absent in certain Roman-based alphabets.</li>
          <li><b>Cross-Lingual Audits:</b> Avoid negative foreign meanings (e.g., <i>"nova-"</i> = "no go" in Spanish) and ensure easy pronounceability.</li>
        </ul>
      </div>
    </div>

    <div class="p-3 rounded-lg bg-emerald-50 dark:bg-emerald-950/40 border border-emerald-200 dark:border-emerald-800 text-base flex items-center gap-3">
      <span class="text-2xl shrink-0">🔬</span>
      <div class="leading-snug text-gray-700 dark:text-gray-200">
        <b>Scope of this Review:</b> Focuses on currently available <b>antibacterials and antifungals</b> (antiviral and antimycobacterial agents are excluded).
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

# The Penicillin Saga: Mold, War, and Secret Codes
<div class="text-gray-400 text-sm -mt-2 mb-4">The discovery that transformed 20th-century medicine</div>

<div class="grid grid-cols-2 gap-6">
  <div>
    <h3 class="text-sm font-bold text-blue-600 dark:text-blue-400 mb-2">Alexander Fleming (1928)</h3>
    <ul class="text-xs space-y-2 text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-300">
      <li>Contaminated staphylococcal culture plate observed with surrounding clearing zones.</li>
      <li>Fungus identified as <i>Penicillium notatum</i> (re-classified in 2011 via whole-genome sequencing as <b><i>Penicillium rubens</i></b>).</li>
      <li><b>Etymology:</b> Latin <i>penicillus</i> = <b>"Paintbrush"</b> or <b>"Broom"</b>, referring to the brush-like branching morphology of the conidiophores under the microscope.</li>
      <li>Suffix <code>-in</code>: Denotes an active chemical constituent or isolated substance.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>

  <div>
    <h3 class="text-sm font-bold text-amber-600 dark:text-amber-400 mb-2">WWII Industrial Miracle</h3>
    <ul class="text-xs space-y-2 text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-300">
      <li>Fleming alone could not mass-produce it; Howard Florey, Ernst Chain, and industrial teams made it clinically viable.</li>
      <li><b>Corn Steep Liquor:</b> Byproduct of corn milling discovered in Peoria, Illinois, increased fermentation yield tenfold.</li>
      <li><b>Brewing Tech:</b> Deep-tank beer fermentation technology allowed Allied troops on D-Day (1944) to carry thousands of doses.</li>
      <li>1945 Nobel Prize shared by Fleming, Florey, and Chain.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="mt-4 p-3 rounded-lg bg-indigo-50 dark:bg-indigo-950/40 border border-indigo-200 dark:border-indigo-800 text-xs">
  <b>The German Espionage Episode:</b> During WWII, German agents requested Fleming's strain from Institut Pasteur in occupied Paris, but were intentionally supplied a decoy strain that produced zero penicillin!
</div>

# Penicillin G vs. Penicillin V: A Secret Code
<div class="text-gray-400 text-sm -mt-2 mb-4">Why is Penicillin V not the 22nd penicillin tested?</div>

<div class="grid grid-cols-2 gap-6 mt-4">
  <div class="p-4 rounded-xl bg-blue-50 dark:bg-blue-950/30 border border-blue-200 dark:border-blue-900">
    <div class="font-bold text-blue-700 dark:text-blue-300 text-base mb-2">Penicillin G (Benzylpenicillin)</div>
    <ul class="text-xs space-y-2 text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-300">
      <li>In the UK, natural penicillins were numbered in Roman numerals (Penicillin I, II, III).</li>
      <li>In the US, they were designated sequentially by letters: <code>A, B, C, D, E, F, G...</code></li>
      <li><b>Penicillin G</b> was simply the 7th sequentially tested natural variant (Penicillin II in the UK).</li>
      <li>Acid-labile $\rightarrow$ degrades in stomach acid; requires IV/IM administration.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>

  <div class="p-4 rounded-xl bg-purple-50 dark:bg-purple-950/30 border border-purple-200 dark:border-purple-900">
    <div class="font-bold text-purple-700 dark:text-purple-300 text-base mb-2">Penicillin V (Phenoxymethylpenicillin)</div>
    <ul class="text-xs space-y-2 text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-300">
      <li><b>The Myth:</b> Many clinicians assume "V" is the 22nd letter in the sequential series.</li>
      <li><b>The Reality:</b> Synthesized in Austria in the 1950s, the "V" stands for the German word <b><i>"Vertraulich"</i></b>, meaning <b>"Confidential"</b> or <b>"Secret"</b>!</li>
      <li>Acid-stable $\rightarrow$ allows oral administration.</li>
      <li><b>"Pen-VK":</b> The "K" denotes the <b>potassium salt</b> (German/Latin: <i>Kalium</i>) for improved solubility.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="mt-4 text-center text-xs font-mono text-gray-500">
  A legendary piece of medical trivia: Penicillin V owes its name to Cold War-era laboratory secrecy!
</div>

# Tailoring the Penicillins: The Prefix Code
<div class="text-gray-400 text-sm -mt-2 mb-4">Chemical engineering to expand spectrum and resist degradation</div>

<div class="grid grid-cols-2 gap-4 text-xs">
  <div class="p-3 rounded-lg bg-gray-50 dark:bg-gray-800/60 border border-gray-200 dark:border-gray-700">
    <div class="font-bold text-indigo-600 dark:text-indigo-400 text-sm">Ampicillin</div>
    <p class="mt-1 text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-300">
      <b>am-</b> (amino group) + <b>-p-</b> (phenyl/benzyl bridge) + <b>-icillin</b>. Adding the amino group enhanced porin penetration through Gram-negative cell walls.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="p-3 rounded-lg bg-gray-50 dark:bg-gray-800/60 border border-gray-200 dark:border-gray-700">
    <div class="font-bold text-indigo-600 dark:text-indigo-400 text-sm">Amoxicillin</div>
    <p class="mt-1 text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-300">
      <b>amox-</b> = <b>a</b>mino + <b>m</b>eth<b>ox</b>y/hydroxy-benzyl group. The hydroxyl modification nearly doubled oral bioavailability over ampicillin (75-90% vs. 40%).
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="p-3 rounded-lg bg-gray-50 dark:bg-gray-800/60 border border-gray-200 dark:border-gray-700">
    <div class="font-bold text-teal-600 dark:text-teal-400 text-sm">Piperacillin</div>
    <p class="mt-1 text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-300">
      <b>piper-</b> refers to the <b>piperazine ring</b> on its ureido side chain. This bulky heterocyclic ring dramatically enhanced affinity for PBP-3 of <i>Pseudomonas aeruginosa</i>.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="p-3 rounded-lg bg-gray-50 dark:bg-gray-800/60 border border-gray-200 dark:border-gray-700">
    <div class="font-bold text-amber-600 dark:text-amber-400 text-sm">Oxacillin / Dicloxacillin</div>
    <p class="mt-1 text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-300">
      <b>oxa-</b> = isoxazolyl ring. <b>di-clox-</b> = two chlorine atoms on the phenyl ring. These bulky substituents create steric hindrance that shields the $\beta$-lactam ring from staphylococcal penicillinase!
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="mt-3 p-2 rounded bg-blue-50 dark:bg-blue-950/30 text-xs text-blue-700 dark:text-blue-300">
  <b>Ticarcillin:</b> <b>tic-</b> comes from <i>thiophene</i> (a sulfur-containing 5-membered ring) + linking element <i>-ar-</i>.
</div>

- When you add an isoxazolyl ring and chlorine atoms, you block penicillinase, giving us oxacillin and dicloxacillin.

# Outsmarting Bacterial Defense: $\beta$-Lactamase Inhibitors
<div class="text-gray-400 text-sm -mt-2 mb-4">The evolution of "-bactams" from Streptomyces to rational design</div>

<div class="space-y-2 text-xs">
  <div class="p-2.5 rounded-lg bg-gray-50 dark:bg-gray-800 border-l-4 border-amber-500 flex justify-between items-start">
    <div>
      <span class="font-bold text-sm text-gray-800 dark:text-gray-100">Clavulanic Acid</span>
      <p class="text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-300 mt-0.5">Isolated in the 1970s from <i>Streptomyces clavuligerus</i>. Suicide inhibitor with negligible antimicrobial activity alone, but restores amoxicillin/ticarcillin activity.</p>
    </div>
    <span class="font-mono bg-amber-100 dark:bg-amber-900/50 text-amber-800 dark:text-amber-300 px-2 py-0.5 rounded text-[11px]">Natural Product</span>
  </div>

  <div class="p-2.5 rounded-lg bg-gray-50 dark:bg-gray-800 border-l-4 border-blue-500 flex justify-between items-start">
    <div>
      <span class="font-bold text-sm text-gray-800 dark:text-gray-100">Sulbactam & Tazobactam</span>
      <p class="text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-300 mt-0.5">
        <b>Sulb-</b> = penicillanic acid <b>sulfone</b>. <b>Tazo-</b> = <b>triazolylmethyl</b> group attached to the sulfone backbone. Suffix <code>-bactam</code> created as a universal contraction for $\beta$-lactamase inhibitor.
      </p>
    </div>
    <span class="font-mono bg-blue-100 dark:bg-blue-900/50 text-blue-800 dark:text-blue-300 px-2 py-0.5 rounded text-[11px]">Synthetic Sulfones</span>
  </div>

  <div class="p-2.5 rounded-lg bg-gray-50 dark:bg-gray-800 border-l-4 border-emerald-500 flex justify-between items-start">
    <div>
      <span class="font-bold text-sm text-gray-800 dark:text-gray-100">Avibactam</span>
      <p class="text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-300 mt-0.5">Non-$\beta$-lactam diazabicyclooctane (DBO) inhibitor. <b>Avi-</b> is derived directly from its early preclinical corporate candidate code: <b>AVE1330A</b>.</p>
    </div>
    <span class="font-mono bg-emerald-100 dark:bg-emerald-900/50 text-emerald-800 dark:text-emerald-300 px-2 py-0.5 rounded text-[11px]">DBO Inhibitor</span>
  </div>

  <div class="p-2.5 rounded-lg bg-gray-50 dark:bg-gray-800 border-l-4 border-purple-500 flex justify-between items-start">
    <div>
      <span class="font-bold text-sm text-gray-800 dark:text-gray-100">Vaborbactam</span>
      <p class="text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-300 mt-0.5">Boronic acid transition-state inhibitor (KPC-targeted). <b>Va-</b> was extracted from the commercial brand name <i>Vabomere</i>, selected for smooth international pronunciation.</p>
    </div>
    <span class="font-mono bg-purple-100 dark:bg-purple-900/50 text-purple-800 dark:text-purple-300 px-2 py-0.5 rounded text-[11px]">Boronic Acid</span>
  </div>
</div>

# Carbapenems: The Antibiotic Heavy Artillery
<div class="text-gray-400 text-sm -mt-2 mb-4">From fragile Thienamycin to stable bedside lifesavers</div>

<div class="grid grid-cols-2 gap-4 text-xs">
  <div class="space-y-3">
    <div class="p-2.5 rounded bg-gray-50 dark:bg-gray-800 border-l-4 border-blue-500">
      <b>Thienamycin:</b> Isolated from <i>Streptomyces cattleya</i> (1970s). Incredible ESBL activity, but instantly degraded in aqueous solution.
    </div>
    <div class="p-2.5 rounded bg-gray-50 dark:bg-gray-800 border-l-4 border-indigo-500">
      <b>The Architecture:</b> <b>Carba-</b> = carbon substituted for sulfur at position 1 of the 5-membered ring + double bond between C2 & C3 $\rightarrow$ <b>-penem</b>.
    </div>
    <div class="p-2.5 rounded bg-gray-50 dark:bg-gray-800 border-l-4 border-emerald-500">

    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="space-y-3">
    <div class="p-3 rounded-lg bg-emerald-50 dark:bg-emerald-950/40 border border-emerald-200 dark:border-emerald-800">

      <p class="mt-1 text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-300">

      <div class="font-bold text-purple-700 dark:text-purple-300 text-sm">Ertapenem (2001)</div>
      <p class="mt-1 text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-300">
        Features a <b>benzoic/carboxylic acid</b> moiety causing negative charge and high albumin binding (>90%). Half-life prolonged to ~4 hours, unlocking <b>once-daily dosing</b> (ideal for OPAT).
      </p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

# Macrolides: Big Rings and Greek Roots
<div class="text-gray-400 text-sm -mt-2 mb-4">Chemical architecture defined by Nobel laureate R.B. Woodward</div>

<div class="grid grid-cols-2 gap-6">
  <div>
    <h3 class="text-sm font-bold text-indigo-600 dark:text-indigo-400 mb-2">The Molecular Architecture</h3>
    <ul class="text-xs space-y-2 text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-300">
      <li><b>Etymology:</b> Greek <i>makros</i> (large) + <i>-lide</i> (lactone). Coined by legendary organic chemist <b>R.B. Woodward</b> to describe large 12- to 16-membered cyclic ester rings.</li>
      <li><b>Pikromycin (1950):</b> The very first recognized macrolide, named from Greek <i>pikros</i> = <b>"bitter"</b>, due to its intensely bitter taste.</li>
      <li><b>Erythromycin (1952):</b> Screened from soil in the Philippines (*Streptomyces erythreus*). Named from Greek <i>erythros</i> = <b>"red"</b> due to its reddish bacterial culture.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>

  <div>
    <h3 class="text-sm font-bold text-teal-600 dark:text-teal-400 mb-2">Second-Generation Refinements</h3>
    <div class="space-y-3 text-xs">
      <div class="p-3 rounded-lg bg-teal-50 dark:bg-teal-950/30 border border-teal-200 dark:border-teal-800">
        <b>Azithromycin (The Azalide):</b>
        <p class="mt-1 text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-300">
          Synthesized by inserting a methyl-substituted nitrogen atom (<b>aza-</b>) directly into the 14-membered lactone ring to create a 15-membered azalide. Eliminates hemiketal formation $\rightarrow$ prevents gastrointestinal cramping and enables massive intracellular tissue retention!

      <div class="p-3 rounded-lg bg-blue-50 dark:bg-blue-950/30 border border-blue-200 dark:border-blue-800">

        <p class="mt-1 text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-300">
          Prefix from Latin <i>clarus</i> = <b>"clear"</b> or <b>"bright"</b>, denoting its refined 6-O-methylation that conferred superior acid stability and a clearer pharmacokinetic profile.
        </p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

# Fluoroquinolones: An Impurity During Malaria Research
<div class="text-gray-400 text-sm -mt-2 mb-4">Serendipity that led to topoisomerase-targeting blockbusters</div>

<div class="grid grid-cols-2 gap-6">
  <div>
    <h3 class="text-sm font-bold text-amber-600 dark:text-amber-400 mb-2">The 1962 Accidental Discovery</h3>
    <ul class="text-xs space-y-2 text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-300">
      <li>George Lesher at Sterling Drug was synthesizing the antimalarial agent <b>chloroquine</b>.</li>
      <li>During distillation, an unexpected chemical byproduct was isolated: <b>Nalidixic acid</b>.</li>
      <li>Weak antibacterial activity, rapid resistance, and CNS/phototoxicity limited early utility.</li>
    </ul>
    <div class="mt-3 p-2.5 rounded bg-blue-50 dark:bg-blue-950/30 text-xs">
      <b>The Fluorine Revolution:</b> Adding a <b>fluorine</b> at C-6 increased DNA gyrase/topoisomerase inhibition >100-fold, creating the <i>fluoroquinolones</i> (stem <code>-floxacin</code>).
    </div>
  </div>

  <div>
    <h3 class="text-sm font-bold text-indigo-600 dark:text-indigo-400 mb-2">Naming Structural Modifications</h3>
    <div class="space-y-2 text-xs">
      <div class="p-2.5 rounded bg-gray-50 dark:bg-gray-800">
        <b>Ciprofloxacin:</b> <b>cipro-</b> = addition of a <b>cyclopropyl</b> group at N-1, maximizing broad-spectrum Gram-negative efficacy.
      </div>
      <div class="p-2.5 rounded bg-gray-50 dark:bg-gray-800">
        <b>Levofloxacin:</b> <b>levo-</b> = pure $L$-enantiomer (active isomer) isolated from the racemic mixture of ofloxacin, cutting the required dose in half and reducing toxicity.
      </div>
      <div class="p-2.5 rounded bg-gray-50 dark:bg-gray-800">

      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

      <li>Isolated from soil collected on the slopes of <b>Mount Ararat in Turkey</b> (*Streptomyces roseosporus*).</li>
      <li><b>Etymology:</b> Hypothesized portmanteau: <b>dap-</b> = <b>d</b>ecanoyl (fatty acid tail) + <b>a</b>mino <b>p</b>eptide core.</li>
      <li><b>Novel MOA:</b> $Ca^{2+}$-dependent insertion of the lipophilic tail into bacterial plasma membrane $\rightarrow$ potassium leakage $\rightarrow$ rapid depolarization without cell lysis.</li>
      <li><i>Warning:</i> Inactivated by pulmonary surfactant!</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</div>

# Oxazolidinones, Lincosamides, and a French Mob Movie
<div class="text-gray-400 text-sm -mt-2 mb-4">The most colorful etymologies in antimicrobials</div>

<div class="grid grid-cols-3 gap-3 text-xs mt-3">
  <div class="p-3 rounded-lg bg-gray-50 dark:bg-gray-800 border-t-4 border-indigo-500">
    <div class="font-bold text-indigo-600 dark:text-indigo-400 text-sm">Linezolid</div>
    <p class="mt-1 text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-300">
      Synthesized by Upjohn in 1993 after DuPont abandoned the class due to myelosuppression.
      <br><br>
      <b>Name breakdown:</b> <b>line-</b> from its morpho<b>line</b> ring + <b>-zolid</b> from the oxa<b>zolid</b>inone core ring.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="p-3 rounded-lg bg-gray-50 dark:bg-gray-800 border-t-4 border-amber-500">
    <div class="font-bold text-amber-600 dark:text-amber-400 text-sm">Clindamycin</div>
    <p class="mt-1 text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-300">
      Parent drug <i>Lincomycin</i> was isolated from <i>Streptomyces lincolnensis</i> in a soil sample from <b>Lincoln, Nebraska</b>.
      <br><br>
      <b>Clindamycin:</b> <b>Cl-</b> indicates the chemical 7-chloro substitution, transforming oral bioavailability from poor to ~90%!
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="p-3 rounded-lg bg-gray-50 dark:bg-gray-800 border-t-4 border-rose-500">
    <div class="font-bold text-rose-600 dark:text-rose-400 text-sm">Rifamycins & Rifampin</div>
    <p class="mt-1 text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-300">
      Isolated in 1957 at Lepetit Laboratories in Milan.
      <br><br>
      <b>The Movie Story:</b> Piero Sensi’s team loved the 1955 classic French crime heist movie <b><i>Rififi</i></b> (French slang for brawl/trouble) and nicknamed the compound <b>Rifomycin</b>!
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="mt-4 p-2 rounded bg-rose-50 dark:bg-rose-950/30 text-xs text-rose-800 dark:text-rose-300 text-center font-mono">
  Rifampin is quite literally named after a 1950s French gangster film!
</div>

# Unintended Triumphs: Accidental Discoveries in the ICU
<div class="text-gray-400 text-sm -mt-2 mb-4">When failed drugs and veterinary agents find second life</div>

<div class="grid grid-cols-2 gap-6 text-xs">
  <div class="p-3.5 rounded-xl bg-emerald-50 dark:bg-emerald-950/30 border border-emerald-200 dark:border-emerald-800">
    <div class="font-bold text-emerald-700 dark:text-emerald-300 text-sm">Metronidazole: The Dental Miracle (1962)</div>
    <p class="mt-1 text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-300">
      Developed solely as an antiparasitic agent for <i>Trichomonas vaginalis</i>.
      <br><br>
      <b>The Serendipitous Case:</b> In 1962, an English woman treated for vaginal trichomoniasis happened to also suffer from severe <b>acute necrotizing ulcerative gingivitis (Vincent's angina / trench mouth)</b>. Both infections cleared simultaneously!
      <br><br>
      This accidental bedside discovery launched metronidazole as the premier antianaerobic agent worldwide.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="p-3.5 rounded-xl bg-amber-50 dark:bg-amber-950/30 border border-amber-200 dark:border-amber-800">
    <div class="font-bold text-amber-700 dark:text-amber-300 text-sm">Fidaxomicin: A Flaw Becomes a Feature</div>
    <p class="mt-1 text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-300">
      Discovered in the 1970s at Abbott Laboratories as <i>Tiacumicin B</i>.
      <br><br>
      <b>The Failure:</b> Abandoned because oral dosing yielded undetectable serum concentrations (zero systemic absorption).
      <br><br>
      <b>The Revival:</b> With the 2000s surge of <i>Clostridioides difficile</i>, near-zero systemic absorption and minimal disruption of normal colonic microbiota made it the ideal first-line agent!
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="mt-3 p-2.5 rounded bg-blue-50 dark:bg-blue-950/30 text-xs text-blue-800 dark:text-blue-300">
  <b>Polymyxins (Colistin & Poly B):</b> Discovered in 1947 from <i>Paenibacillus polymyxa</i> (<b>myx-</b> = slimy exudate). Feared and shelved for decades due to severe nephrotoxicity; resurrected in the 2000s as our absolute last resort against pan-drug-resistant GNB.
</div>

# The Antifungal Frontier: Polyenes (A Tale of Two Women)
<div class="text-gray-400 text-sm -mt-2 mb-4">Nystatin and Amphotericin B: Ergosterol binding and toxicity</div>

<div class="grid grid-cols-2 gap-6">
  <div class="p-4 rounded-xl bg-pink-50 dark:bg-pink-950/30 border border-pink-200 dark:border-pink-800">
    <div class="font-bold text-pink-700 dark:text-pink-300 text-sm mb-1">Nystatin (1949): Born in Albany, NY</div>
    <ul class="text-xs space-y-2 text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-300">
      <li>Discovered by two female scientists: microbiologist <b>Elizabeth Hazen</b> and biochemist <b>Rachel Brown</b>.</li>
      <li>Originally named <i>"fungicidin"</i>.</li>
      <li><b>Etymology:</b> Renamed in honor of their employer:
        <div class="my-1 font-mono text-center text-sm font-bold text-pink-600 dark:text-pink-400">
          Ny + sta + tin = New York State Department of Health!
        </div>
      </li>
      <li>Hazen and Brown donated all royalties ($13+ million) to establish scientific research grants for women.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>

  <div class="p-4 rounded-xl bg-blue-50 dark:bg-blue-950/30 border border-blue-200 dark:border-blue-800">
    <div class="font-bold text-blue-700 dark:text-blue-300 text-sm mb-1">Amphotericin B: "Amphoteric" Nature</div>
    <ul class="text-xs space-y-2 text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-300">
      <li>Isolated in 1953 from soil along the Orinoco River, Venezuela (*Streptomyces nodosus*).</li>
      <li><b>Etymology:</b> Named for its <b>amphoteric</b> chemical behavior (possessing both acidic carboxyl and basic amino groups).</li>
      <li><b>"Amphoterrible":</b> Infusion reactions and renal tubular damage. Decades later, Lipid Complex (ABLC) and Liposomal (L-AmB) formulations encapsulated the drug, sparing nephrons while preserving fungicidal potency.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</div>

# Azoles & Glucan Synthase Inhibitors: Precision Mycology
<div class="text-gray-400 text-sm -mt-2 mb-4">Targeting the fungal cell membrane and wall</div>

<div class="grid grid-cols-2 gap-4 text-xs mt-2">
  <div class="p-3 rounded-lg bg-gray-50 dark:bg-gray-800 border-l-4 border-indigo-500">
    <div class="font-bold text-indigo-600 dark:text-indigo-400 text-sm">The Azole Family</div>
    <ul class="mt-1 space-y-1.5 text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-300">
      <li><b>-conazole</b> suffix standardized after Miconazole.</li>
      <li><b>Fluconazole:</b> <b>flu-</b> = two fluorine atoms on the benzene ring.</li>
      <li><b>Voriconazole:</b> <b>vori-</b> = trifluorinated adaptation.</li>
      <li><b>Isavuconazole:</b> <b>isa-</b> = <b>isomer</b> (features a unique 2,5-difluorophenyl substitution rather than the traditional 2,4-difluorophenyl).</li>
    </ul>
  </div>

  <div class="p-3 rounded-lg bg-gray-50 dark:bg-gray-800 border-l-4 border-emerald-500">
    <div class="font-bold text-emerald-600 dark:text-emerald-400 text-sm">Echinocandins & Triterpenoids</div>
    <ul class="mt-1 space-y-1.5 text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-300">
      <li><b>Echinocandins:</b> Greek <i>echin-</i> = <b>"spiny"</b> or <b>"sea urchin"</b> (*Aspergillus nidulans var. echinulatus* conidiophore morphology).</li>
      <li><b>Caspofungin:</b> <b>cas-</b> = dual activity against <b>C</b>andida and <b>As</b>pergillus.</li>
      <li><b>Ibrexafungerp:</b> First-in-class triterpenoid glucan synthase inhibitor. Suffix <b>-erp</b> represents triterpenoids.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="mt-4 p-2.5 rounded bg-purple-50 dark:bg-purple-950/30 text-xs">
  <b>Flucytosine (5-FC):</b> Synthesized in 1957 as an antimetabolite for leukemia. Failed as an anticancer drug, but was brilliantly repurposed in the 1960s to treat cryptococcal meningitis.
</div>

# Conclusion & The Future of Antimicrobials
<div class="text-gray-400 text-sm -mt-2 mb-4">Lessons from 80 years of antimicrobial discovery</div>

<div class="space-y-3 text-xs">
  <div class="p-3 rounded-lg bg-blue-50 dark:bg-blue-950/30 border-l-4 border-blue-500">
    <div class="font-bold text-blue-700 dark:text-blue-300 text-sm">Every Name Is an Archive</div>
    <p class="text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-300 mt-1">
      Antimicrobial names commemorate wartime desperation, geographic locations from Borneo to Nebraska, laboratory secrecy (<i>Penicillin V</i>), and pure serendipity (<i>Metronidazole, Rifampin</i>).
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="p-3 rounded-lg bg-emerald-50 dark:bg-emerald-950/30 border-l-4 border-emerald-500">
    <div class="font-bold text-emerald-700 dark:text-emerald-300 text-sm">The Paradigm Shift in Drug Discovery</div>
    <p class="text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-300 mt-1">
      From classical <b>soil screening & serendipity</b> $\rightarrow$ to <b>chemical derivatization</b> $\rightarrow$ to modern <b>rational molecular modeling, CRISPR, and multi-omics</b>.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="p-3 rounded-lg bg-rose-50 dark:bg-rose-950/30 border-l-4 border-rose-500">
    <div class="font-bold text-rose-700 dark:text-rose-300 text-sm">The Never-Ending Arms Race</div>
    <p class="text-gray-600 dark:text-gray-300 mt-1">
      As ICU clinicians facing rising pan-drug resistance (MDR/PDR), antimicrobial stewardship and rational drug design remain our strongest weapons in this eternal evolutionary war.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>
